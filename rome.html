THE HISTORY OF ROME
IN FILM & SERIES

From founding myth to final fall — the complete watch order

753 BC — 1453 AD
Phase I
753 BC
The Mythical Founding
Romulus & Remus · twin brothers suckled by a she-wolf · fratricide · first wall of Rome built · the Seven Hills settled · Romulus becomes the first king
FILM The First King (2019) — Il Primo Re
SERIES Romulus — Season 1 (2020)
SERIES Romulus — Season 2 (2022)
⟡ Start here — this is the very seed of Rome
⟡ The First King is raw, visceral, in ancient Latin dialect
⟡ Romulus (Sky Atlantic) expands the founding myth beautifully
Phase II
753 – 509 BC
The Roman Kingdom
Seven Kings of Rome · the Rape of the Sabine Women · Tarquinius Superbus, the last tyrannical king · Lucretia's violation sparks the revolution that ends the monarchy and births the Republic
FILM Duel of Champions (1961)
FILM The Rape of the Sabine Women (1961)
FILM The Last Glory of Troy (1962)
⟡ These are Italian peplum films — optional but atmospheric
⟡ Duel of Champions covers the Horatii vs Curiatii duel, ~670 BC
Phase III
509 – 400 BC
Birth of the Republic
Expulsion of kings · Senate established · early patrician-plebeian class wars · the Conflict of the Orders · Coriolanus, the exiled general who turned enemy of Rome
FILM Coriolanus (2011)
⟡ Ralph Fiennes directs & stars — set in early Republic, ~490 BC
⟡ Captures the aristocratic arrogance vs populist tension perfectly
⚔ Character Spotlight — Coriolanus
Shakespeare's Coriolanus is set ~490 BC — a Roman general so contemptuous of the common people he leads the enemy against Rome. Fiennes's modern retelling is the only major screen version. Watch before moving on.
Phase IV
264 – 146 BC
The Punic Wars — Rome vs Carthage
Three wars against Carthage · Hannibal Barca crosses the Alps with war elephants · Battle of Cannae, Rome's worst defeat · Scipio Africanus turns the tide at Zama · Carthage is razed to the ground · Rome becomes master of the Mediterranean
MINI Hannibal (2005) — Alexander Siddig
FILM Hannibal (1959) — Victor Mature
FILM Cabiria (1914) — silent epic, 2nd Punic War
FILM Scipio Africanus (1937) — Italian epic, Zama
⟡ Watch Hannibal (2005) first — most accurate and complete arc
⟡ Cabiria (1914) is a cinematic landmark, optional but stunning
🐘 Character Spotlight — Hannibal Barca
History's greatest general crossed the Alps with 37 elephants. Hannibal (2005) miniseries gives him the most dignified and complete screen treatment. Watch this to understand who terrified Rome most in the Republic era.
Phase V
133 – 82 BC
Crisis of the Republic
Gracchi brothers assassinated for land reform · Marius and Sulla's civil wars · Sulla becomes the first Roman to march an army into Rome · the Republic cracks under political violence and military dictatorship
DOC Mary Beard's Ultimate Rome (2012)
DRAMA Barbarians Rising (2016) — Eps. 1–2
⟡ No major drama covers Gracchi / Marius / Sulla yet
⟡ Mary Beard's documentary fills this gap perfectly
⟡ Barbarians Rising Ep.1 gives tribal context to Rome's border pressures
Phase VI
73 – 71 BC
Spartacus — The Slave Uprising
Thracian gladiator Spartacus escapes Capua and raises an army of 120,000 slaves · defeats eight Roman armies · marches the length of Italy · ultimately crushed by Crassus · 6,000 slaves crucified along the Appian Way
MINI Spartacus: Gods of the Arena (2011) — WATCH FIRST
SERIES Spartacus: Blood and Sand — Season 1 (2010)
SERIES Spartacus: Vengeance — Season 2 (2012)
SERIES Spartacus: War of the Damned — Season 3 (2013)
FILM Spartacus (1960) — Kubrick / Kirk Douglas ⟡ parallel
⟡ Gods of the Arena is the prequel — sets up Batiatus's ludus
⟡ Watch Kubrick's Spartacus (1960) alongside S2 as the classic alternative
⟡ Crassus & a young Julius Caesar both appear — notice them carefully
⚔ Character Spotlight — Spartacus, Crassus, Caesar
Crassus (Starz series: Simon Merrells) is depicted crushing the revolt. He and Pompey will both claim credit. A young Caesar briefly appears. These are the men who will soon carve Rome apart in the Phase VIII civil war.
Phase VII
100 – 49 BC
Caesar's Rise & the First Triumvirate
Julius Caesar rises through the cursus honorum · forms the First Triumvirate with Crassus and Pompey · Gallic Wars — Caesar conquers modern-day France in eight campaigns · crosses the Rubicon into civil war in 49 BC
MINI Julius Caesar (2002) — Jeremy Sisto
SERIES Rome — Season 1, Eps. 1–4 (2005)
⟡ Julius Caesar (2002) covers his full rise from youth
⟡ Rome S1 Ep.1–4 opens at the end of the Gallic Wars, 52 BC
⟡ Rome follows Vorenus & Pullo — two soldiers who witness history firsthand
Phase VIII
49 – 44 BC
Caesar's Civil War & Dictatorship
Caesar vs Pompey · Battle of Pharsalus · Pompey flees to Egypt and is murdered · Caesar meets Cleopatra in Alexandria · Caesar's dictatorship · the Ides of March, 44 BC — senators Brutus & Cassius lead the assassination · 23 stab wounds
SERIES Rome — Season 1, Eps. 5–12 (2005)

⟡ Cleopatra’s Egypt continues in egypt.html — use that file for the full Ptolemaic arc
👑 Character Spotlight — Julius Caesar
Ciaran Hinds (Rome), Jeremy Sisto (2002 mini), and Rex Harrison (Cleopatra 1963) each give you a facet of Caesar. Rome S1 shows his brilliance and ruthlessness through the eyes of soldiers. The 1953 Brando version is essential for the assassination aftermath.
SERIES Rome S1 — Hinds as Caesar
FILM Julius Caesar (1953) — Marlon Brando as Antony
Phase IX
44 – 27 BC
The Second Triumvirate & Final Civil Wars
Antony, Octavian, and Lepidus divide the world · Brutus & Cassius defeated at Philippi · Antony takes the East and falls for Cleopatra · Octavian & Antony's final conflict · Battle of Actium 31 BC · Antony and Cleopatra's suicides · Octavian stands alone — the Republic is over

♛ Character Spotlight — Cleopatra VII
Watch both halves of Cleopatra (1963) as your parallel track through Phases VIII and IX. Elizabeth Taylor's Cleopatra is the definitive screen portrayal. Cleopatra (1999) gives more political depth. For Antony's descent, Rome S2 + Antony and Cleopatra (1972) together are unbeatable.
FILM Cleopatra (1963) — Taylor & Burton
MINI Cleopatra (1999) — Leonor Varela
Phase X
27 BC – 14 AD
Augustus — The First Emperor
Octavian becomes Augustus — "the revered one" · Pax Romana begins · the Principate established · Rome's golden age · Virgil, Horace, Ovid flourish · Augustus's wife Livia maneuvers the succession · his heirs die mysteriously until Tiberius is chosen
MINI Augustus: The First Emperor (2003) — Peter O'Toole
SERIES Domina — Season 1 (2021)
SERIES Domina — Season 2 (2023)
⟡ Augustus (2003): Peter O'Toole narrates his life in old age — masterful
⟡ Domina follows Livia — watch S1 simultaneously with Augustus (2003)
♛ Character Spotlight — Livia Drusilla
The most powerful woman in Roman history. Domina (Sky Atlantic) gives her the full arc — from a teenage bride to the empress-mother who may have killed half the imperial family. Watch both Domina seasons before proceeding. Then continue in I, Claudius where she appears as an old woman still wielding iron control.
SERIES Domina S1 & S2 — Kasia Smutniak
SERIES I, Claudius — Sian Phillips as Livia
Phase XI
14 – 68 AD
The Julio-Claudian Dynasty
Tiberius — paranoid and reclusive · Caligula — divine madness · Claudius — the unexpected scholar-emperor · Nero — artistic, narcissistic, burns Rome · the Julio-Claudian line ends in blood and fire
SERIES I, Claudius — All 13 Eps. (BBC, 1976) ← ESSENTIAL

👁 Character Spotlight — Caligula
John Hurt's Caligula in I, Claudius (1976) is the greatest screen performance of the mad emperor — contained, chilling. Malcolm McDowell's Caligula (1979) is controversial and explicit but gives his full excess and fall. Watch Hurt first, then McDowell as supplemental.
SERIES I, Claudius Ep.6–8 — John Hurt

Phase XII
64 – 96 AD
Nero's Fall, Year of Four Emperors & The Flavians
Rome burns in 64 AD · Nero blames Christians · Nero's suicide · civil war — four emperors in one year: Galba, Otho, Vitellius, Vespasian · Vespasian founds the Flavian dynasty · Titus destroys Jerusalem in 70 AD · Vesuvius obliterates Pompeii and Herculaneum in 79 AD · Domitian's reign of terror ends the dynasty
FILM Pompeii (2014) — volcanic spectacle, 79 AD
DRAMA Pompeii: The Last Day (BBC, 2003) ← more accurate

Phase XIII
96 – 138 AD
The Five Good Emperors — Nerva to Hadrian
Nerva · Trajan — Rome reaches its greatest territorial extent · Dacian Wars · the Column of Trajan still stands · Hadrian consolidates — builds his wall across Britain · the Empire at its most sophisticated, stable, and vast
FILM Centurion (2010) — Neil Marshall, Hadrian's frontier
FILM The Eagle (2011) — Hadrian's Britain, ~140 AD
FILM The Last Legion (2007) — stretches to this era's memory
⟡ Watch Centurion + The Eagle back-to-back — both set on the frontier
⟡ Centurion (2010) covers the legendary disappearance of the Ninth Legion ~117 AD
⟡ The Eagle (2011) follows a soldier recovering his father's lost legion's standard
🦅 Character Spotlight — Hadrian's Soldiers
No major film has been made about Hadrian or Trajan themselves — a great gap in Roman cinema. The Eagle and Centurion cover the texture of frontier life beautifully. The isolation, the cold, and the empire's edges are well captured. Watch both as a double feature.
Phase XIV
161 – 192 AD
Marcus Aurelius & Commodus — Peak & Rupture
Marcus Aurelius — philosopher-emperor, author of the Meditations · wages endless wars against Germanic tribes · his son Commodus is everything he is not — vain, cruel, delusional · Commodus declares himself Hercules reborn · assassinated 192 AD · the Antonine golden age dies with him
FILM The Fall of the Roman Empire (1964) — WATCH FIRST
FILM Gladiator (2000) — Ridley Scott ← WATCH SECOND
FILM Gladiator II (2024) — Ridley Scott, Caracalla era ⟡ after
⟡ The Fall of the Roman Empire (1964): Alec Guinness as Marcus Aurelius
⟡ Both films cover the SAME historical moment — watch 1964 film first
⟡ Gladiator (2000) takes creative liberty but captures the spirit perfectly
⟡ Gladiator II is set in the Severan era ~211 AD — a generation later
👑 Character Spotlight — Marcus Aurelius & Commodus
Alec Guinness (1964) gives you a noble, weary philosopher-king. Richard Harris (Gladiator 2000) gives you the same man's final haunted hour. Joaquin Phoenix's Commodus is cinema gold — the petulant, jealous son who destroys his father's legacy. These two films are the best back-to-back watch in this entire curriculum.
Phase XV
193 – 284 AD
The Crisis of the Third Century
Year of the Five Emperors · the Severan dynasty · Caracalla grants citizenship to all free Romans · 50 years of chaos — over 20 emperors · economic collapse · the plague of Cyprian kills millions · barbarian invasions on every border · the empire nearly dissolves
DRAMA Barbarians Rising (2016) — History Channel, Eps. 3–5
DOC Fall of Civilizations — Roman Ep.
⟡ No major drama covers this era — documentary bridge is recommended
⟡ Barbarians Rising gives the barbarian perspective during Rome's fragmentation
⟡ Gladiator II is also loosely set early in this era (~211–218 AD Caracalla)
Phase XVI
284 – 337 AD
Diocletian's Reform & Constantine's Revolution
Diocletian splits the empire into the Tetrarchy · stabilizes Rome through iron rule · the Great Persecution of Christians · Constantine the Great defeats his rivals · sees a vision of the cross before the Battle of Milvian Bridge 312 AD · issues the Edict of Milan — Christianity becomes legal · Constantinople founded 330 AD · Rome has a new heart, a new religion, a new direction
FILM Constantine and the Cross (1961) — Cornel Wilde
DOC Constantine's Sword (2007) — documentary
⟡ Constantine and the Cross (1961) is the major screen treatment
⟡ Covers his rise, the Milvian Bridge, and conversion to Christianity
✝ Character Spotlight — Constantine the Great
Constantine's conversion is one of history's most consequential moments. It transformed Rome, birthed Christendom, and shaped Western civilization for 1,700 years. No modern prestige production exists yet — Constantine and the Cross (1961) remains the primary screen version, imperfect but functional.
Phase XVII
337 – 395 AD
Christian Rome & the Pagan Sunset
Sons of Constantine divide the empire · Julian the Apostate attempts to reverse Christianity — fails · Theodosius I makes Christianity the official state religion · pagan temples closed · Hypatia of Alexandria and the last flames of classical learning · the empire is formally divided in 395 AD — East and West, never to be reunited
FILM Agora (2009) — Rachel Weisz as Hypatia
⟡ Agora is set in Alexandria ~391–415 AD — watch it here
⟡ Depicts the violent Christian destruction of the Library and pagan learning
⟡ One of the most underrated Roman-era films ever made
📜 Character Spotlight — Hypatia of Alexandria
Agora (2009) is one of the most intellectually powerful films in this entire curriculum. Rachel Weisz plays the philosopher-mathematician caught between Roman paganism and the rising tide of Christian fanaticism. Essential watching for understanding what Rome became at its spiritual crossroads.
Phase XVIII
395 – 455 AD
Attila the Hun & the Western Collapse
The Western Empire weakens under child-emperors and corrupt generals · Visigoths sack Rome in 410 AD — the first time in 800 years · Attila the Hun, "the Scourge of God," ravages Gaul and Italy · Pope Leo I rides out to negotiate Rome's survival · the Vandals sack Rome in 455 AD — two sackings in 45 years
MINI Attila (2001) — Gerard Butler / Powers Boothe
FILM Sign of the Pagan (1954) — Jack Palance as Attila
DRAMA Barbarians Rising (2016) — Attila episodes
⟡ Attila (2001) miniseries is the best modern treatment — Powers Boothe as Aetius
⟡ Gerard Butler plays Attila with genuine menace
⟡ Watch Attila (2001) then Sign of the Pagan (1954) as the classic comparison
⚔ Character Spotlight — Attila & Aetius
Attila and the Roman general Aetius grew up together as hostages in each other's courts — and then became the two greatest enemies of the age. The 2001 miniseries captures this tragic irony beautifully. Aetius is sometimes called "the last of the Romans." These two men are the final great drama of the Western Empire.
Phase XIX
455 – 476 AD
The Final Fall — The Last Western Emperor
A procession of puppet emperors controlled by barbarian generals · Odoacer, a Germanic chieftain, deposes Romulus Augustulus on 4 September 476 AD · no battle, no dramatic last stand — just a quiet deposition · he sends the imperial regalia to Constantinople · the Western Roman Empire is over after 500 years
FILM The Last Legion (2007) — Colin Firth, Ben Kingsley
DRAMA Barbarians Rising (2016) — Odoacer final episodes
⟡ The Last Legion follows Romulus Augustulus, the last Western emperor
⟡ Takes creative liberties but captures the pathos of Rome's twilight perfectly
⟡ Watch it as the emotional close to the Western story
🌅 The Last Roman Moment
Romulus Augustulus was a teenager when he became the last emperor. He was deposed without being killed — Odoacer pitied him. He lived out his days in a castle in Campania. The Western Roman Empire didn't end with a great battle. It ended with a sad, quiet letter to Constantinople. The Last Legion dramatises this poignantly.
Phase XX
476 – 565 AD
The Eastern Roman Empire — Justinian's Reconquest
The Eastern Empire (Byzantium) survives · Constantinople — the New Rome · Emperor Justinian I and Empress Theodora — the most remarkable imperial couple in history · Justinian reconquers North Africa, Italy, and parts of Spain · the great Hagia Sophia is built · the Justinianic Plague devastates the empire · Belisarius, the greatest general of the age
DOC Byzantium: The Lost Empire (TLC, 1997) — Eps. 1–2
FILM Teodora, Imperatrice di Bisanzio (1954)
⟡ Byzantium: The Lost Empire (1997) is a 4-episode documentary — essential
⟡ John Romer guides through Constantinople, Ravenna & the lost world of Byzantium
⟡ Eps. 1–2 cover the founding through Justinian's golden age
👑 Character Spotlight — Justinian & Theodora
Theodora was born a circus performer — she became Empress of Rome. Justinian was the last Roman emperor who spoke Latin as a first language. Together they were the last great flame of the ancient world. Shockingly, no prestige modern series exists about them yet. Byzantium: The Lost Empire (doc) fills the gap until one does.
Phase XXI
565 – 1204 AD
Byzantium — The Long Vigil
Centuries of survival against Arabs, Bulgars, Normans, and Seljuks · the Macedonian dynasty's golden age · the Great Schism of 1054 — Eastern and Western Christianity divide forever · the First through Fourth Crusades pass through Constantinople · 1204 AD — Crusaders sack Constantinople in the Fourth Crusade, a catastrophe from which Byzantium never truly recovers
DOC Byzantium: The Lost Empire — Eps. 3–4 (1997)
DRAMA Barbarians Rising — later episodes for context

⟡ Very little drama covers this middle Byzantine period — documentary is your bridge
Phase XXII
1204 – 1453 AD
The Last Century of Rome — Fall of Constantinople
A weakened, shrunken Byzantine state · the Ottomans rise and surround Constantinople · 21-year-old Sultan Mehmed II assembles the largest army of the medieval world · a 53-day siege in 1453 AD · the great Theodosian Walls breached by the world's largest cannons · Emperor Constantine XI dies fighting on the walls · the last Roman Emperor falls · Rome's 2,206-year story ends — 29 May 1453 AD

🗡 The Last Roman Emperor — Constantine XI
Constantine XI Palaiologos had no money, no fleet, only 7,000 men against 80,000 Ottomans. He refused to surrender. He died fighting in the streets, his imperial regalia discarded so he would not be captured alive. His body was never found. He is the last Emperor of Rome. Rise of Empires: Ottoman gives him the dignity he deserves.
